Isomerism & Stereochemistry

Class 11 | JEE Advanced

1. Chapter summary

Isomers share a molecular formula but differ in structure. Constitutional (structural) isomers differ in atom connectivity: chain, position, functional, metamerism, ring-chain and tautomerism. Stereoisomers have the same connectivity but differ in spatial arrangement. They are either configurational (geometrical and optical, interconverted only by breaking bonds) or conformational (interconverted by rotation about σ bonds, in dynamic equilibrium).

Symmetry elements (σ, i, Sn) decide whether a molecule is chiral, and the counting formulas depend on whether the molecule is symmetrical. The stereochemical outcome of a reaction (syn/anti addition, inversion/retention, anti-periplanar E2) is decided by its mechanism.

Degree of unsaturation (DBE)

DBE = C − (H + X)/2 + N/2 + 1 (O and S are ignored). DBE = (number of rings) + (number of π bonds), and a triple bond counts as 2. Isomers must have the same DBE, but it does not tell you which of ring or π bond is present.

C6H64Benzene (3 π + 1 ring) and many open-chain poly-ynes/enynes

Alkane isomer counts: C4H10 = 2, C5H12 = 3, C6H14 = 5.

Keto-enol tautomerism

Requires at least one α-H. HCHO, PhCHO and (CH3)3C−CO−C(CH3)3 have none, so they cannot enolise. Tautomers are different compounds in equilibrium, unlike resonance structures, which are only different drawings of one species. For simple carbonyls the keto form wins, because C=O + C−H is more stable than C=C + O−H.

3. Conformational analysis

Conformations are arrangements related by rotation about single bonds. Dihedral (torsional) angle is the angle between two bonds on adjacent atoms viewed down the central bond. Torsional strain is repulsion between eclipsed bond pairs. Steric strain is van der Waals repulsion between bulky groups held close together.

5. Optical isomerism & stereochemical relationships

Symmetry elementDescriptionEffect on chirality
Plane of symmetry (POS, σ)A plane dividing the molecule into two mirror-image halves (equivalent to S1)Molecule is achiral
Centre of symmetry (COS, i)A point such that every atom has an identical atom directly opposite at equal distance (equivalent to S2)Molecule is achiral
Alternating axis (Sn)Rotate by 360°/n, then reflect in the plane perpendicular to the axis; if the result is identical, Sn is presentMolecule is achiral
Simple axis (Cn)Rotation by 360°/n gives an identical moleculeDoes not decide chirality. Chiral molecules can have C2 axes (e.g. trans-1,2-dimethylcyclopropane).

A molecule is chiral if and only if it has no Sn axis (no σ, no i, no S4 etc.). Dissymmetric = chiral with no Sn.

CIP rules for R/S

  • Priority goes to the higher atomic number at the first point of difference. For isotopes, higher mass wins (D > H).
  • Multiple bonds are treated as duplicated atoms. Examples: −COOH > −CHO > −CH2OH, and −C≡CH > −CH=CH2 > −CH(CH3)2 > −CH2CH3.
  • Orient the lowest-priority group away from you. Priorities 1→2→3 clockwise = R, anticlockwise = S.
  • R/S has no fixed relation to the sign of rotation, (+)/(−), or to D/L.

Stereochemical relationships

RelationshipDefinitionProperties
EnantiomersNon-superimposable mirror images; all stereocentres invertedIdentical m.p., b.p., solubility and spectra in achiral media. Equal and opposite rotation. React differently with chiral reagents.
DiastereomersStereoisomers that are not mirror images (includes cis/trans isomers)Different physical properties, so they can be separated by ordinary methods
EpimersDiastereomers that differ at exactly one stereocentreE.g. D-glucose and D-mannose (C-2)
Meso compoundContains stereocentres but is achiral through an internal POS or COSOptically inactive by internal compensation; cannot be resolved
Racemic mixture1:1 mixture of enantiomersOptically inactive by external compensation; can be resolved

Resolution of a racemate: convert it into diastereomeric salts with a chiral resolving agent and separate by fractional crystallisation, use chiral chromatography, or use enzymes.

Enantiomeric excess: ee (%) = |R − S| / (R + S) × 100. Optical purity = [α]observed / [α]pure × 100, which equals ee when rotation is proportional to composition. Percentage of the major enantiomer = 50 + ee/2. Example: a sample with 70% R and 30% S has ee = 40%.

Pseudoasymmetric centres

A centre bearing four different ligands, of which two are enantiomorphic (one R-type, one S-type). It is labelled with lower-case r or s. In the CIP rule for such cases the (R)-ligand ranks higher than the (S)-ligand. The molecule can be meso. Example: C-3 of pentane-2,3,4-triol.

CHECK: the full CIP tie-break sequence (like pairs RR/SS rank above unlike pairs RS, then R > S) is longer than the shorthand above. Verify against the CIP statement your syllabus follows.

Stereospecific vs stereoselective

Stereospecific: different stereoisomeric reactants give different stereoisomeric products, and the mechanism fixes the outcome. Stereoselective: one stereoisomer is preferred over another regardless of the reactant's stereochemistry. All stereospecific reactions are stereoselective; the reverse is not true. Example: dehydrohalogenation of 2-bromobutane favouring trans-but-2-ene is stereoselective but not stereospecific.

7. Common traps and exceptions

trans-Cyclooctene is chiral. The twisted eight-membered ring gives a stable chirality (planar/axial) with no chiral carbon, and the enantiomers do not interconvert at room temperature. CHECK: whether your book calls this planar or axial chirality.
Gauche can beat anti. In 2-fluoroethanol, ethylene glycol and 1,2-difluoroethane the gauche conformer is more stable (H-bond and hyperconjugation). Do not assume anti is always lowest in energy.
Pseudoasymmetric centre: the (R)-ligand outranks the (S)-ligand, and the centre is labelled r/s. Pentane-2,3,4-triol has two meso forms that differ only at C-3, and they are diastereomers, not enantiomers.
Cumulenes flip the rule: even number of C=C (allene) gives optical isomerism; odd number (butatriene) gives geometrical isomerism.
Biphenyls need bulky ortho groups. Small groups (F, H, OH) allow rotation, so 2,2′-difluorobiphenyl is not resolvable. If either ring has identical groups at both ortho positions, the molecule has a mirror plane and is achiral. For example, 2,2′,6,6′-tetranitrobiphenyl is achiral.
Do not mix up labels: chiral centres do not guarantee a chiral molecule (meso). Racemic is not meso. R/S has no fixed relation to (+)/(−). Optical activity belongs to the whole molecule, not to a single centre.

8. Practice questions

Q1 (conceptual). Calculate the DBE of C4H8 and state how many structural isomers it has, and how many in total if cis/trans isomers are counted.

DBE = 4 − 8/2 + 1 = 1 (one ring or one C=C). Structural isomers: but-1-ene, but-2-ene, 2-methylpropene, cyclobutane, methylcyclopropane = 5. But-2-ene has cis and trans forms, so the total is 6.

Q2 (conceptual, multiple correct). Which can show optical activity? (A) CH3CH=C=CHCH3 (B) CH3CH=C=C=CHCH3 (C) CH3CH=C=C(CH3)2 (D) (4-methylcyclohexylidene)acetic acid

Answer: A and D. (A) is an allene with H and CH3 on each end: chiral. (B) has three cumulative double bonds, so it shows GI, not optical activity. (C) has two identical CH3 on one terminal carbon, so it is achiral. (D) shows axial chirality like an allene.

Q3 (conceptual). meso-Tartaric acid has two chiral centres but is optically inactive. Explain, and say whether it can be resolved.

It is (2R,3S). In the eclipsed conformation it has a plane of symmetry (in the staggered anti conformation, a centre of symmetry), so the molecule is achiral. The rotations of the two halves cancel internally (internal compensation). It is a single achiral compound, so it cannot be resolved, unlike racemic (±)-tartaric acid.

Q4 (conceptual). Why does acetylacetone contain far more enol than acetone, and why does the enol content drop in water?

The acetylacetone enol is stabilised by conjugation of C=C with the second C=O and by a six-membered intramolecular H-bonded chelate ring. Its α-H, flanked by two C=O groups, is also more acidic and easier to remove. Acetone has none of these. In water, solvent molecules H-bond to the C=O and the enol OH, competing with the chelate, so the keto form is favoured. In non-polar solvents the chelate persists and the enol content is high.

Q5 (counting). How many stereoisomers does pentane-2,3,4-triol have? Classify them.

4. C-2 and C-4 are stereocentres; C-3 is stereogenic only when C-2 and C-4 have opposite configurations (pseudoasymmetric). (2R,4R) and (2S,4S) are an enantiomeric pair (C-3 is not a stereocentre in them). (2R,3r,4S) and (2R,3s,4S) are two meso diastereomers. Formula for symmetrical n = 3: 22 = 4 total, 2 meso, 1 pair.

Q6 (counting). How many geometrical isomers does CH3−CH=CH−CH=CH−CH=CH−CH3 (octa-2,4,6-triene) have?

6. Symmetrical polyene with n = 3 (odd): 22 + 21 = 6. Explicitly: EEE, ZZZ, EEZ (= ZEE), EZE, ZEZ, ZZE (= EZZ).

Q7 (order). Arrange the n-butane conformers in decreasing stability: fully eclipsed, gauche, anti, partially eclipsed. Give the CH3/CH3 dihedral angle of each.

Anti (180°) > gauche (60°) > partially eclipsed (120°) > fully eclipsed (0°). Anti has no strain; gauche has steric strain only; partially eclipsed has torsional strain; fully eclipsed has torsional plus large steric strain from the CH3/CH3 contact.

Q8 (tricky, multiple correct). Which are correct? (A) cis-1,2-Dimethylcyclohexane has one axial and one equatorial CH3 in each chair. (B) The more stable chair of trans-1,4-dimethylcyclohexane has both CH3 equatorial. (C) cis-1,3-Dimethylcyclohexane is more stable than trans-1,3-dimethylcyclohexane. (D) trans-1,4-Dimethylcyclohexane is chiral.

Answer: A, B, C. (A) cis-1,2 is axial,equatorial in both chairs. (B) trans-1,4 can be equatorial,equatorial. (C) cis-1,3 can be equatorial,equatorial while trans-1,3 always has one axial CH3 (about 7.5 kJ/mol less stable). (D) is false: trans-1,4 has a plane of symmetry and is achiral.

Q9 (tricky, multi-step). cis- and trans-But-2-ene are each treated separately with (i) Br2/CCl4 and (ii) cold dilute alkaline KMnO4. Name the stereochemical form of each of the four products, and say how many of the isolated products are optically active.

(i) Br2 adds anti: cis gives the racemic (2R,3R)/(2S,3S) dibromide; trans gives meso. (ii) KMnO4 adds syn: cis gives meso-butane-2,3-diol; trans gives the racemic diol. Meso compounds are inactive by internal compensation and racemates by external compensation, so none of the four isolated products is optically active.

Q10 (tricky). How many stereoisomers does CH3−CH=CH−CH(OH)−CH=CH−CH3 (hepta-2,5-dien-4-ol) have?

4. Each double bond can be E or Z. (E,E) and (Z,Z): the two arms on C-4 are identical, so C-4 is not a stereocentre and each is a single achiral compound (2 isomers). (E,Z): the two arms differ, so C-4 becomes a stereocentre and gives an enantiomeric pair (2 isomers). Total = 2 + 2 = 4.
