General Organic Chemistry (GOC)

Class 11 | JEE Advanced

1. Chapter summary

GOC explains where electrons sit and how they move in organic molecules. Electronic effects (inductive, resonance, hyperconjugation, electromeric) decide the stability of molecules and intermediates. Stability of carbocations, carbanions, free radicals and carbenes decides the reaction path. The same effects fix acidity and basicity orders. Nomenclature and hybridisation give the structural language for the rest of organic chemistry.

2. Electronic effects

EffectNatureKey points
Inductive (I)Permanent, through σ bondsFalls rapidly with distance (negligible after about 3 carbons). Depends on electronegativity and on s-character of the attached carbon (sp > sp2 > sp3 in electron withdrawal).
Resonance / mesomeric (R, M)Permanent, through π bonds / lone pairsNeeds conjugation and a planar arrangement. Resonance structures differ only in electron positions, never atom positions. More equivalent structures give more stabilisation.
HyperconjugationPermanent, σ(C-H) with adjacent empty p or π orbital"No-bond resonance". Number of hyperconjugative structures = number of α-H. Stabilises alkenes, carbocations and radicals.
Electromeric (E)Temporary, only in presence of an attacking reagentComplete transfer of the π pair to one atom of a multiple bond. +E: transfer towards the attacked atom's side (e.g. with H+); −E: transfer away from the attacking reagent.

SituationDominant effect

4. Acidity and basicity orders

ClassOrderReason
Carboxylic acids (−I groups)HCOOH > CH3COOH. Cl3CCOOH > Cl2CHCOOH > ClCH2COOH > CH3COOH. FCH2COOH > ClCH2COOH > BrCH2COOH > ICH2COOH.−I groups stabilise the carboxylate anion; more halogens and nearer halogens give a greater effect. Alkyl (+I) groups destabilise it. HCOOH has no +I alkyl group.
Phenols2,4,6-trinitrophenol > 2,4-dinitrophenol > p-nitrophenol > o-nitrophenol (intramolecular H-bond) > m-nitrophenol > phenol > p-cresolNitro group at o/p stabilises phenoxide by −M and −I. At meta only −I works. Alkyl and OCH3 (at para) raise electron density on O and decrease acidity.

Substituted benzoic acids (ortho effect)Almost any ortho substituent makes the acid stronger than benzoic acid, whether donating or withdrawing. Example: o-toluic > benzoic > p-toluic. With −NO2: ortho > para ≈ meta > benzoic.Steric inhibition of resonance: the ortho group twists −COOH out of the ring plane, so the ring cannot donate electrons into it. This increases acidity. o-hydroxybenzoic acid is also strengthened by intramolecular H-bond stabilising the anion.
Substituted benzoic acids (para/meta)p-nitro or m-nitro > benzoic > p-OCH3 ≈ p-CH3−M/−I groups stabilise the anion; +M/+I groups destabilise it.
Amines (aqueous basicity)(CH3)2NH > CH3NH2 > (CH3)3N > NH3. For ethyl: (C2H5)2NH > (C2H5)3N > C2H5NH2 > NH3. Aromatic: p-toluidine > aniline > p-nitroaniline. Aliphatic amines > NH3 > aniline.Combined effect of +I (stabilises R3NH+) and solvation (H-bonding, falls from 1° to 3°) and steric hindrance. Aniline's lone pair is delocalised into the ring. Ortho-substituted anilines (any group) are weaker bases than aniline (ortho effect).
Hybridisation of Nsp3 amine > sp2 (pyridine-type) > sp (nitrile)More s-character holds the lone pair closer to the nucleus, so it is less available.

5. Nucleophiles, electrophiles and reaction types

TermDescriptionExamples
NucleophileElectron-rich species that donates an electron pairAnions: OH−, OR−, CN−, I−, enolates. Neutral: NH3, H2O, ROH, R3N. Ambident: CN−, NO2−, enolate (C or O attack).
ElectrophileElectron-poor species that accepts an electron pairCations: H+, NO2+, R+. Lewis acids: AlCl3, BF3, FeCl3. Neutral: SO3, carbonyl carbon (δ+), carbene.
Nucleophilicity trendsAlong a period: decreases with electronegativity (NH2− > OH− > F−). Down a group in protic solvents: I− > Br− > Cl− > F−. In polar aprotic solvents the order reverses toward basicity (F− most reactive). A negatively charged species is more nucleophilic than its neutral conjugate acid.
Reaction typeWhat happens
Substitution (SN1, SN2, SNi, SEAr)One atom/group replaced by another. SN1: carbocation, racemisation, 3° favoured. SN2: one step, inversion, 1° favoured.
Addition (electrophilic, nucleophilic, radical)Reagent adds across a multiple bond. Alkenes: electrophilic. C=O: nucleophilic.
Elimination (E1, E2, E1cB)Loss of small molecule (HX, H2O) to form a multiple bond. Zaitsev product is usually major.
RearrangementAtom/group migrates within the molecule (e.g. carbocation shifts, pinacol-pinacolone).
Bond fissionHowProducts and conditions
HomolyticEach atom keeps one electron of the bondFree radicals. Non-polar solvent, light, heat, peroxides. Typical of non-polar bonds.

7. Common traps and exceptions

Halogens are odd: −I > +M, so they deactivate the ring, but the +M still makes them ortho/para-directing. For para-halobenzoic acids, p-F is a weaker acid than p-Cl because 2p-2p overlap makes +M stronger for F.
Carbocation and carbanion orders are opposite. Alkyl groups help cations (+I, hyperconjugation) and hurt anions. Among alkyl carbanions, CH3− is the most stable.
Aromaticity beats the usual order: cyclopentadiene is unusually acidic (aromatic anion); cyclopentadienyl cation is antiaromatic and very unstable.
Bridgehead cations cannot be planar in small bicyclic systems, so they are very unstable even if tertiary. Resonance needs planarity.
Ortho effect works against the usual order: an electron-donating ortho group can still increase acidity of benzoic acid, and decrease basicity of aniline. Do not apply para/meta logic to ortho.
Meta position cannot use resonance: m-nitrophenol is less acidic than p-nitrophenol. Also, amides are not basic since the N lone pair is delocalised onto C=O, and pyrrole is not basic for the same reason.
CHECK: relative stability of allyl and benzyl cations against 2° and 3° alkyl cations is context-dependent (gas phase vs solution). Prefer the order stated in your book rather than a single universal order.

8. Practice questions

Q1 (conceptual). Count the hyperconjugative (no-bond) structures for 2-methylbut-2-ene, (CH3)2C=CH−CH3.

Answer: 9. Count the α-H on carbons attached to the C=C: two CH3 on C2 give 6, and the CH3 on C3 gives 3. Total 9.

Q2 (conceptual, multiple correct). In which of these is the central carbon sp2-hybridised? (A) CH3+ (B) CH3− (C) CH3• (D) singlet :CH2

Answer: A, C, D. CH3+ is trigonal planar (sp2). CH3• is taken as planar sp2 in the standard treatment. Singlet carbene is bent sp2. CH3− is pyramidal sp3.

Q3 (conceptual). Why is p-nitrophenol more acidic than phenol, while p-methoxyphenol is less acidic?

The p-NO2 group stabilises the phenoxide ion by −M and −I (negative charge delocalised onto nitro O atoms). At para, OCH3 shows +M > −I, so it pushes electron density onto the phenoxide O and destabilises it.

Q4 (conceptual). Why is aniline a weaker base than ammonia and than cyclohexylamine?

The N lone pair of aniline is delocalised into the benzene ring by resonance, so it is less available to accept H+. The anilinium ion has no such stabilisation. Cyclohexylamine has +I groups and no delocalisation, so it is a stronger base than NH3.

Q5 (order). Arrange in decreasing acidity: CH3COOH, ClCH2COOH, Cl2CHCOOH, ICH2COOH.

Cl2CHCOOH > ClCH2COOH > ICH2COOH > CH3COOH. More −I halogens stabilise the carboxylate better; Cl has a stronger −I than I.

Q6 (order). Arrange in decreasing aqueous basicity: (CH3)3N, (CH3)2NH, CH3NH2, NH3, C6H5NH2.

(CH3)2NH > CH3NH2 > (CH3)3N > NH3 > C6H5NH2. +I raises basicity, but 3° amines lose from poor solvation of the cation and steric hindrance. Aniline is weakest due to resonance delocalisation.

Q7 (order). Arrange in decreasing acidity: phenol, p-nitrophenol, m-nitrophenol, p-cresol.

p-nitrophenol > m-nitrophenol > phenol > p-cresol. p-NO2 acts by −M and −I; m-NO2 only by −I; the methyl group of p-cresol donates electrons (+I and hyperconjugation) and weakens acidity.

Q8 (tricky, multiple correct). Which statements are correct? (A) o-toluic acid is more acidic than benzoic acid. (B) p-toluic acid is more acidic than benzoic acid. (C) o-nitrobenzoic acid is more acidic than p-nitrobenzoic acid. (D) p-methoxybenzoic acid is less acidic than benzoic acid.

Answer: A, C, D. (A) is the ortho effect (steric inhibition of resonance). (B) is false: p-CH3 donates electrons, so p-toluic acid is weaker. (C) is true: −I of the nearby NO2 is strongest at ortho, plus the ortho effect. (D) is true: +M of OCH3 at para dominates.

Q9 (tricky). The cation (CH3)2CH−CH+−CH3 rearranges. Give the product cation and its number of α-H.

A 1,2-hydride shift moves H from the tertiary carbon to the cationic carbon, giving the tertiary cation (CH3)2C+−CH2CH3 (2-methylbutan-2-yl cation). α-H count: two CH3 (6 H) plus CH2 (2 H) = 8.

Q10 (tricky). Give the IUPAC name of CH2=CH−CH(OH)−CH2−COOH and the hybridisation of C-4.

The principal group is −COOH (C1), then C2 (CH2), C3 (CHOH), C4 (=CH), C5 (=CH2). Name: 3-hydroxypent-4-enoic acid. C-4 is part of C=C, so it is sp2. The OH is only a prefix because the acid group has higher priority.
