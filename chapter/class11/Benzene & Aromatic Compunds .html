Benzene & Aromatic Compounds

Class 11 • Core Organic Chemistry Focus Area

1. Chapter Summary

This chapter centers around the fundamental concept of aromaticity, characterized by unusual structural stability resulting from a continuous cyclic delocalised cloud of (4n + 2) π-electrons (Hückel's Rule). The primary chemical behavior of these systems is dominated by Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution (SEAr) reactions rather than addition reactions, ensuring the highly stable aromatic core remains intact. Advanced JEE problems typically test the multi-step synthetic sequences involving regioselective substituent orientation, activation/deactivation matrices, and competing side-chain pathways.

2. Reaction Mechanisms

Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution (SEAr) General Scheme

  1. Step 1: Generation of the Electrophile (E+) — Achieved using specialized Lewis/Brønsted acid catalysts.
  2. Step 2: Attack of the Aromatic π System on E+ — Formation of a resonance-stabilised carbocation intermediate, known as the Sigma Complex or Arenium Ion. Aromaticity is temporarily disrupted here. (Rate-Determining Step - RDS)
  3. Step 3: Deprotonation by a Base — Rapid loss of H+ from the sp3 hybridised carbon atom to restore the aromatic system.

Favoured by: Stronger electron-donating groups attached to the ring (+R, +I effects), which stabilise the positive charge build-up in the Arenium Ion transition state.

Friedel-Crafts Alkylation & Acylation Rearrangements

  • Alkylation: Electrophile is a carbocation (R+). Whenever possible, 1° or 2° carbocation complexes undergo 1,2-hydride or 1,2-methyl shifts to yield a more stable carbocation before attacking the aromatic ring.
  • Acylation: Electrophile is the acylium ion (R-C+=O ↔ R-C≡O+). This ion is exceptionally resonance-stabilised and does not undergo skeletal carbocation rearrangements, making acylation an effective route for constructing unbranched side chains via subsequent reduction.
