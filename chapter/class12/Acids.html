Acids

Class 12 | JEE Advanced

Core ideas

Carboxylic acids contain the –COOH functional group and combine the chemistry of a carbonyl group with that of an O–H group. Their characteristic reactions include acid-base reactions, nucleophilic acyl substitution, esterification, formation of acid derivatives, reduction, decarboxylation and reactions at the α-carbon.

The most important JEE ideas are acidity and its substituent effects, resonance stabilisation of the carboxylate ion, relative reactivity of acid derivatives, mechanisms of nucleophilic acyl substitution, the Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky reaction, decarboxylation, and conversions between carboxylic acids and their derivatives.

Common traps

Acidity is not controlled by the stability of the neutral acid alone. Compare the stability of the conjugate bases. Resonance delocalisation in RCOO− is a major reason carboxylic acids are much more acidic than alcohols.
Inductive effect is distance-dependent. An electron-withdrawing group on the α-carbon affects acidity much more strongly than the same group several bonds away.
Fischer esterification is reversible. Conc. H2SO4 acts as an acid catalyst and can also help remove water. Driving the equilibrium toward ester can involve excess alcohol or removal of water.
Amide is unusually unreactive. Resonance donation from nitrogen gives the C–N bond partial double-bond character and reduces the electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon.
HVZ requires an α-hydrogen. A carboxylic acid with no α-H cannot undergo the standard Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky α-halogenation pathway.
Soda-lime decarboxylation removes one carbon. RCOONa → RH means the carbonyl carbon is lost as CO2. This is different from reactions that retain the carbonyl carbon.
Kolbe electrolysis is not ordinary decarboxylation. Two carboxylate-derived radicals are generated at the anode and generally couple, giving R–R. The organic product therefore contains two R fragments.
Hunsdiecker gives a one-carbon-shorter alkyl halide. The silver carboxylate loses CO2, and the resulting radical is trapped by halogen.
Benzoic acid is not simply an aliphatic carboxylic acid with a phenyl substituent. Aromatic-ring substituents influence acidity through both inductive and resonance effects, so position must be considered carefully.

Practice questions

1. Conceptual: Why is a carboxylic acid much more acidic than the corresponding alcohol?The conjugate base of a carboxylic acid is a carboxylate ion, in which the negative charge is delocalised over two oxygen atoms by resonance. The alkoxide ion has the negative charge localised mainly on one oxygen. Greater conjugate-base stability gives greater acidity.
2. Conceptual: Why does an electron-withdrawing group on the α-carbon increase the acidity of a carboxylic acid more strongly than the same group farther away?The effect is primarily inductive. Inductive electron withdrawal decreases rapidly with distance, so an EWG close to the –COOH group stabilises the negatively charged carboxylate ion more effectively.
3. Conceptual: Why is an amide less reactive toward nucleophilic acyl substitution than an ester?Nitrogen donates electron density into the carbonyl by resonance, reducing the electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon. In addition, the amide leaving group would be a much poorer leaving group than an alkoxide under comparable conditions.
4. Conceptual: Why does Kolbe electrolysis generally produce a product containing twice the alkyl fragment?At the anode, a carboxylate ion loses CO2 and an electron to generate an alkyl radical, R•. Two such radicals couple to form R–R. Thus two identical alkyl fragments are joined.
5. Product prediction: What is the major organic product when sodium propanoate is heated with soda lime?Propane, CH3CH2CH3. Sodium propanoate loses its carboxyl carbon as CO2, so the product has one fewer carbon atom.
6. Product prediction: What is formed when CH3CH2COOH undergoes the Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky reaction followed by hydrolysis?2-Bromopropanoic acid, CH3CHBrCOOH. The α-carbon is brominated because propanoic acid contains an α-hydrogen.
7. Product prediction: What is obtained from sodium acetate by Kolbe electrolysis?Ethane, CH3–CH3. Acetate loses CO2 to form CH3•, and two methyl radicals couple.
8. Multi-step: Convert CH3CH2COOH to CH3CH2CH2OH. State a suitable reagent and explain why an aldehyde is not isolated as the final product.Use LiAlH4 in dry ether followed by acidic work-up. Carboxylic acids are reduced beyond the aldehyde stage under these strongly reducing conditions, giving the primary alcohol, propan-1-ol.
9. Comparison: Arrange HCOOH, CH3COOH, ClCH2COOH and CCl3COOH in increasing order of acidity.CH3COOH < HCOOH < ClCH2COOH < CCl3COOH. The methyl group is electron donating, formic acid lacks that electron-donating alkyl group, and increasing chlorine substitution strongly stabilises the conjugate base through the −I effect.
10. Multi-step/comparison: Arrange CH3COCl, (CH3CO)2O, CH3COOCH3 and CH3CONH2 in decreasing reactivity toward nucleophilic acyl substitution, and identify the key structural reason.CH3COCl > (CH3CO)2O > CH3COOCH3 > CH3CONH2. The trend mainly follows the ability of the group attached to the acyl carbon to leave and the extent of resonance donation into the carbonyl.
