Common traps

Chlorobenzene resists nucleophilic substitution: sp2 C-Cl is shorter and stronger, resonance gives partial double bond character, and the phenyl cation is unstable. An -NO2 at ortho/para position enables SNAr (addition-elimination).
Ambident nucleophiles (CN-, NO2-): ionic salts (KCN, KNO2) give R-CN and R-ONO, while covalent salts (AgCN, AgNO2) attack through nitrogen, giving R-NC and R-NO2.

Practice questions

1. Which reacts fastest by SN1: CH3Cl, CH3CH2Cl, (CH3)2CHCl, (CH3)3CCl?(CH3)3CCl, as it forms the most stable (tertiary) carbocation.
2. (R)-2-bromobutane reacts with NaOH by SN2. What is the product's configuration?(S)-butan-2-ol: complete inversion of configuration.
3. Product of CH3CH2Br with AgCN?Ethyl isocyanide (CH3CH2NC), because covalent AgCN attacks through nitrogen.
4. Major product of 2-bromopentane with alc. KOH?Pent-2-ene (Saytzeff product), mainly the trans isomer.
5. Why does the Finkelstein reaction go to completion?NaCl/NaBr is insoluble in dry acetone and precipitates, shifting the equilibrium (Le Chatelier).
6. Why is chlorobenzene less reactive than chloroethane toward nucleophiles?Partial double bond character from resonance, sp2 carbon, and repulsion between the nucleophile and the ring's pi cloud.
