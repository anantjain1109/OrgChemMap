Aldehydes and Ketones

Class 12 | JEE Advanced

Core ideas

Aldehydes (R-CHO) and ketones (R-CO-R') contain the polar carbonyl group. Resonance makes the carbonyl carbon electrophilic, so the characteristic reaction is nucleophilic addition. Aldehydes are generally more reactive toward nucleophilic addition than ketones because they have less steric hindrance and fewer electron-releasing alkyl groups. The carbonyl group also controls acidity at the α-carbon, enol/enolate chemistry, oxidation, and condensation reactions.

For JEE Advanced, connect four ideas: carbonyl electrophilicity, enolate formation, oxidation/reduction, and selective reactions of aldehydes versus ketones.

Comparisons and orders

Carbonyl reactivity toward nucleophilic addition:
HCHO > aliphatic aldehydes > ketones. Reason: aldehydes have less steric hindrance and fewer +I alkyl groups. For substituted aromatic carbonyl compounds, electronic effects of substituents must also be considered.
Acidity of α-hydrogens:
Carbonyl compounds with α-H are appreciably more acidic than ordinary alkanes because the conjugate base is resonance-stabilised as an enolate. Electron-withdrawing substituents increase α-H acidity; electron-donating alkyl groups generally decrease it.
Enolate stability:
The enolate is resonance-stabilised between oxygen and the α-carbon. More substituted alkyl groups do not automatically mean a more stable enolate; kinetic versus thermodynamic enolate formation depends on base, temperature, solvent and reversibility.
Aldehyde oxidation susceptibility:
Aldehydes are much more readily oxidised than ketones because the aldehyde carbonyl carbon bears hydrogen and can be converted directly into a carboxylic-acid oxidation state.
Basicity/nucleophilicity of carbonyl oxygen:
The carbonyl oxygen has lone pairs and can be protonated or coordinate to Lewis acids. Electron-donating substituents increase electron density at oxygen, whereas electron-withdrawing groups decrease it.
Acid-catalysed carbonyl hydration:
More electrophilic carbonyl compounds generally hydrate more readily. Formaldehyde is especially susceptible; steric and electronic effects control equilibria for substituted carbonyls.

Common traps and exceptions

Trap 1: Cannizzaro is not the normal reaction of every aldehyde. A prerequisite for the classical self-Cannizzaro reaction is absence of an α-hydrogen.
Trap 2: Aldol condensation requires an enolizable carbonyl compound in the normal pathway. Benzaldehyde, for example, has no α-H and does not undergo self-aldol condensation.
Trap 3: Tollens' reagent is not an absolutely exclusive aldehyde test. Other reducing substances can reduce Ag(I), so identify the complete reaction context.
Trap 4: Fehling's solution and Tollens' reagent are not interchangeable in all cases. Aromatic aldehydes commonly fail Fehling's test while reducing Tollens' reagent.
Trap 5: Grignard reagents are destroyed by acidic -OH/-NH/-SH groups and by water. Dry ether and anhydrous conditions are essential before the carbonyl reaction.
Trap 6: Clemmensen and Wolff-Kishner both convert C=O to CH2, but their conditions are opposite in character: Clemmensen is strongly acidic, whereas Wolff-Kishner is strongly basic.
Trap 7: Haloform requires a methyl-ketone unit, CH3CO-R, or a compound that is oxidised to such a unit under the reaction conditions. Not every ketone gives the haloform test.
Trap 8: Friedel-Crafts acylation does not normally suffer from the rearrangement characteristic of carbocations because the acylium ion is resonance-stabilised.
Trap 9: In crossed aldol reactions, several products may be possible when both carbonyl partners have α-hydrogen. Selectivity requires analysing which enolate and which electrophile are actually generated.

Practice questions

1. Conceptual: Why is ethanal generally more reactive toward nucleophilic addition than propanone?Ethanal has only one alkyl group and one hydrogen attached to the carbonyl carbon, whereas propanone has two alkyl groups. Ethanal therefore has both lower steric hindrance and a smaller +I electron-releasing effect, leaving its carbonyl carbon more electrophilic.
2. Conceptual: Why does benzaldehyde undergo Cannizzaro reaction but acetaldehyde normally undergoes aldol reaction under basic conditions?Benzaldehyde has no α-hydrogen, so it cannot form the normal enolate required for aldol chemistry and instead can undergo Cannizzaro disproportionation. Acetaldehyde has α-hydrogen and readily forms an enolate, allowing aldol chemistry.
3. Conceptual: Why must a Grignard reaction be performed under anhydrous conditions?RMgX is strongly basic and reacts with water or other acidic protons to form RH, destroying the carbon nucleophile. Consequently, moisture prevents efficient addition to the carbonyl compound.
4. Conceptual: Both Clemmensen and Wolff-Kishner reductions convert a carbonyl group into CH2. Why might one be chosen over the other?Clemmensen uses strongly acidic Zn(Hg)/HCl conditions, whereas Wolff-Kishner uses strongly basic hydrazine/KOH and heat. The choice depends on which functional groups in the substrate tolerate the required conditions.
5. Product prediction: Predict the major product when ethanal is treated with dilute NaOH followed by heating.CH3CHO first undergoes aldol addition to give CH3CH(OH)CH2CHO. On heating, dehydration gives CH3CH=CHCHO (but-2-enal/crotonaldehyde), with the more stable E isomer generally favoured.
6. Product prediction: Predict the product of benzaldehyde with concentrated KOH.2 C6H5CHO → C6H5CH2OH + C6H5COO-K+. Benzaldehyde has no α-H, so it undergoes Cannizzaro disproportionation.
7. Product prediction: What is obtained when acetophenone is treated with I2/NaOH followed by acidification?Acetophenone undergoes the haloform reaction: C6H5COCH3 → C6H5COO- + CHI3. On acidification, the benzoate gives benzoic acid and CHI3 remains as the yellow haloform precipitate.
8. Multi-step: Convert CH3CH2OH to CH3CH2CHO without intentionally oxidising the aldehyde further. Give a suitable sequence.Use controlled dehydrogenation/oxidation of ethanol under conditions that stop at the aldehyde stage, such as Cu at elevated temperature for dehydrogenation: CH3CH2OH → CH3CHO + H2. Strong excess oxidants would risk further oxidation to ethanoic acid.
9. Comparison: Arrange HCHO, CH3CHO, CH3COCH3 and (CH3)2CO in increasing order of reactivity toward a typical nucleophile, and justify.For the simple aliphatic series, ketones are less reactive than aldehydes, and formaldehyde is the most reactive because it has no alkyl groups and minimal steric hindrance. Thus a standard order is (CH3)2CO < CH3CHO < HCHO. The two ketone names in the question are identical, so they are equal.
10. Multi-step/comparison: A compound A gives a positive Tollens' test, undergoes aldol condensation, and on Clemmensen reduction gives ethylbenzene. Identify A.A must contain an aldehyde carbonyl and an α-hydrogen. Clemmensen reduction changes RCHO to RCH3. To give ethylbenzene, the corresponding aldehyde is C6H5CH2CHO (3-phenylpropanal would instead give propylbenzene). Therefore the stated target and conditions are inconsistent with a unique aldehyde satisfying all clues.
